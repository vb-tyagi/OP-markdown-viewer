# on keeping a notebook

i have kept a notebook for eleven years and i still can't tell you what it is for.

it isn't a diary. the days are mostly missing. it isn't a planner, because the plans in it were rarely followed. it isn't a commonplace book either, although there are quotes in the margins that i copied down and never looked at again.

the closest description i have is this: the notebook is a place where noticing becomes a habit.

## what goes in

almost anything. a phrase overheard on a train. the way a shop owner folded a paper bag. a question i couldn't answer at dinner.

> the point isn't to keep the thought. the point is to have had it properly.

the act of writing something down forces a second look. the second look is where most of the value lives.

## what stays out

i try not to write about my mood. mood is loud enough already, and it crowds out the small things the notebook is good at catching.

i also keep tasks out. a task list wants to be finished, and the notebook should never feel finished.

## why it works

nobody reads it. that's the whole trick. writing with no audience is the only time the sentences don't perform, and unperformed sentences are where you find out what you actually think. as a friend once put it, “a notebook is a room with the door closed.”
